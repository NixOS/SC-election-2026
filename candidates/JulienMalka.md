#### What I will do

I am running with one main intent: give the project functional governance. I have [explained in the past what structural issues make this very challenging](https://luj.fr/notes/20260809T111918==public--steering-committee-report-aug-9-2026__steeringcommittee.org). Without change, we will always leave it up to elections to give us SC members competent and dedicated enough to keep the boat "afloat". I am advocating for structural changes in our constitution that will fix the issues in our governance (lack of delegation, micromanagement of teams, etc.) *by design*. If you vote for me, that will be my main focus. Although I have concrete ideas of directions for structural changes (for example forcing team representation in the SC decision process or composition), I want those structural changes to emerge from discussion with all stakeholders, so I am intentionally not putting forward any concrete proposal here. If I fail to gather enough votes for structural changes, I will work on culture.

Structurally changing our governance is hard (5/7 votes required to change the constitution). If you vote for me, also consider voting for:

Situations where teams feel some amount of micromanagement from the SC should never happen in a project where the bulk of the work is done by volunteers. For that reason, the SC must adhere to the spirit of delegation described in the constitution and limit its "top-down" decision making to exceptional cases. In the two first years of SC exercises, this "ideal" vision has not been followed in practice. We can fix this with proper culture, sure, but I think we should make structural changes too. I tend to believe those changes should go in the direction of "forcing some amount of teams representation in the SC decision-making process". Like @lassulus, I like the Rust Leadership Council.
